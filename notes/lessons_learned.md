# Lessons Learned

## Trading Strategy

- Strong simple signals can be more useful than complicated models.
- Visual inspection is very helpful for quickly understanding market structure.
- A strategy needs execution logic, not only a directional signal.

## Risk Control

- Position limits are not just constraints; they shape the whole strategy.
- Inventory-aware quoting is important for market making.
- Maximizing expected value without considering risk can lead to large losses.

## Backtesting

- Backtesting assumptions matter a lot.
- A strategy can look good if it accidentally exploits a small sample pattern.
- It is important to separate robust signals from overfitted observations.

## Implementation

- Logging and visualization make debugging much easier.
- Parameter tuning should be systematic when time allows.
- Special-case logic can be powerful but must be used carefully.

## Manual Challenges

- Manual rounds often require predicting other teams, not just solving a math problem.
- Game-theoretic reasoning and behavioral assumptions both matter.
